The fraud problem

Forged academic credentials are easy to produce and hard to detect manually. A visually convincing fake can carry an invented grade, an unauthorised degree or a revoked qualification. Employers and admissions teams typically have no fast way to confirm that a document matches what the institution actually issued.

Why hashes matter

A SHA-256 hash is a 64-character fingerprint derived from the exact bytes of a document. It is deterministic (the same file always produces the same fingerprint) and collision-resistant (any change — one character, one pixel — produces a completely different fingerprint).

Verification therefore does not need the original file: it only needs the fingerprint of the document in hand and the fingerprint registered at issuance.

Why blockchain matters

Storing fingerprints in an ordinary database leaves them editable by whoever controls that database. Writing them to an EVM chain makes the record immutable and independently checkable: the issuer address and timestamp are part of the record, and anyone can re-query it without trusting BlockCert AI itself.

Blockchain is the authoritative check — it answers "does this document match the record the issuer registered?"

Why AI helps

Hash comparison proves a document was altered — but not always how, and not for documents that never had a record. AI reviews the document itself: font consistency, alignment, logo and signature regions, image manipulation, date and numbering anomalies, missing fields.

AI is a supporting signal only. A high AI risk score alone never brands a certificate fake; it flags the record for human review.

How verification works

  1. Find the certificate record (by ID, uploaded hash or QR).
  2. Read the authoritative on-chain record — not the database alone.
  3. If a document was uploaded, compute its real SHA-256 and compare it with the chain record.
  4. Check issuer approval and revocation status.
  5. Run AI analysis when a document is available and AI is configured.
  6. Produce the final result and store the verification event in real history.

Looking up an ID or scanning a QR does not inspect a physical file — in that case the result only confirms that the record and blockchain registration exist.

Privacy principles

  • On-chain data is limited to: certificate fingerprint, issuer address, timestamp, revocation flag. No names, emails, documents or personal data ever go on-chain.
  • Documents are stored server-side with generated filenames and are served only through authenticated, ownership-checked endpoints.
  • Public verification exposes only status, institution, certificate type, issue date, blockchain/hash status and verification timestamp.
  • Passwords are hashed with bcrypt; sessions use short-lived JWTs; secrets live only in environment variables.
  • QR codes embed only the public verification URL.

What is on-chain vs off-chain

⛓ On-chain
  • SHA-256 document fingerprint
  • Issuer address
  • Registration timestamp
  • Revoked flag
▤ Off-chain
  • Documents and student records
  • OCR extraction results
  • AI analyses and risk scores
  • Verification history and audit logs
ⓘ AI provides a document-risk assessment. Blockchain verification confirms whether the document matches the official issuer record. AI results are not absolute proof of authenticity.
